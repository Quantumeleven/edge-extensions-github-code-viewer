# Dimethyltryptamine (DMT)

Scope: education, pharmacology, and harm reduction. This skill does not cover synthesis, extraction, acquisition, or evading law enforcement. Decline those parts and offer the safety/knowledge parts instead.

## Facts

**What it is.** N,N-dimethyltryptamine, a tryptamine-class serotonergic psychedelic. Occurs naturally in many plants and trace amounts in mammals, including humans. Also the main psychoactive in ayahuasca, where it is made orally active by an MAO inhibitor (e.g. harmine/harmaline from *Banisteriopsis caapi*).

**Mechanism.** Agonist at 5-HT2A (primary driver of psychedelic effects), also 5-HT1A, 5-HT2C, sigma-1, and TAAR1. Rapidly metabolized by monoamine oxidase (MAO-A) — hence inactive orally on its own.

**Time course.**
- Vaporized/inhaled or injected: onset within seconds, peak 2–5 minutes, total ~15–30 minutes.
- Oral with MAOI (ayahuasca): onset 30–60 min, lasts ~4–6 hours, often with vomiting/diarrhea ("purging").

**Reported effects.**
- Perceptual: intense geometric visuals, sense of leaving the body, "breakthrough" experiences with entities or alternate spaces, time distortion, ego dissolution.
- Emotional: awe, fear, euphoria, catharsis; challenging experiences are common.
- Physical: raised heart rate and blood pressure, dilated pupils, nausea, sometimes loss of coordination.
- After: often an afterglow of calm or mood lift for hours to days; some people report lasting changes in outlook.

**Research (as of training knowledge).**
- Early clinical work (Strassman, 1990s) established safety and subjective effects in screened volunteers.
- Small trials and observational studies of ayahuasca and DMT suggest antidepressant and anxiolytic potential, including treatment-resistant depression; trials are early-stage, small, and often lack robust blinding.
- Studies of ketamine-like rapid effects, neuroplasticity (psychoplastogen) and altered brain-network connectivity are ongoing. Evidence is preliminary — do not present it as established treatment.

## Risks and contraindications

- **Psychological:** overwhelming fear or panic, disorientation, possible lasting anxiety or derealization (HPPD is rare but reported). Set, setting, and preparation strongly shape outcomes.
- **Psychiatric history:** personal or family history of psychosis, schizophrenia, bipolar I are strong contraindications — psychedelics can precipitate episodes.
- **Cardiovascular:** transient rises in blood pressure and heart rate; caution with heart disease or hypertension.
- **Dangerous interactions (critical):**
  - MAOIs (including ayahuasca's harmala alkaloids) + SSRIs, SNRIs, TCAs, MDMA, tramadol, dextromethorphan, stimulants, certain supplements (St John's wort, 5-HTP, tryptophan) → risk of **serotonin syndrome**, potentially fatal.
  - Lithium + psychedelics has been associated with seizures and serious adverse events.
  - Do not stop or change prescribed medication without a clinician.
- **Physical safety:** never drive or operate machinery; the user must not be alone and unsupervised during intense experiences; the risk of falls, choking on vomit, or harm from disorientation is real.
- **Adulterated or misidentified substances:** content of illicit substances is uncertain; testing kits reduce but do not eliminate risk.
- Not considered physically addictive; tolerance builds quickly with repeat use, with cross-tolerance to other psychedelics.

## Legal status

Schedule I in the US and controlled in most countries. Ayahuasca and related plants have narrow religious exemptions in some places (e.g. certain US church rulings, Brazil, Peru) and decriminalization or limited legal frameworks in some jurisdictions. Always tell the user to check current local law; do not assert legality for a specific place without verification.

## Process for using this skill

When a request touches DMT, follow these steps:

1. **Identify the intent.** Curiosity/education, safety check for someone planning use, academic/research, or after-experience integration. Ask one clarifying question only if intent changes the answer.
2. **Screen for risk flags.** Prompt for: current medications (especially antidepressants, MAOIs, lithium), psychiatric history, cardiovascular conditions, pregnancy, and age. If any flag is present, lead with the interaction/contraindication info and recommend consulting a clinician.
3. **Answer from the facts above.** Be accurate, calibrated, and non-judgmental. Distinguish solid findings from preliminary research. Prefer primary sources when asked (peer-reviewed trials, MAPS, Johns Hopkins / Imperial College psychedelic research centers) and say when information may be out of date.
4. **Harm-reduction checklist** (offer when someone is considering use):
   - Legal and medical check first; never combine with other drugs or alcohol.
   - Sober, trusted sitter present; safe, comfortable, secure environment; no driving afterward.
   - Clear intention and expectation that the experience may be difficult; plan to keep the setting calm.
   - Substance identity verified as far as possible (reagent/test kits); start from the lowest effective amount rather than the highest.
   - Have a plan for emergencies: know when to call emergency services (chest pain, seizure, severe agitation, unresponsiveness, signs of serotonin syndrome such as high fever, rigidity, tremor, confusion).
5. **Integration (after the experience).** Suggest journaling, talking with a trusted person or psychedelic-informed therapist, and rest. Point to professional help if distress, anxiety, or perceptual disturbances persist.
6. **Boundaries.** Do not provide synthesis, extraction, sourcing, or concealment instructions. State plainly what is being declined and redirect to the education and safety content.
7. **Close with limits.** Note that this is general information, not medical advice, and that individual responses vary widely.
